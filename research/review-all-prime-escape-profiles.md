# Independent review of exponent-escape and signed fixed-support argument

**Verdict:** The deductions in [all-prime-escape-profiles.md](all-prime-escape-profiles.md) are valid as conditional structural restrictions on a hypothetical infinite counterexample family. I found no uniform finiteness argument in them. The source-dependent all-prime reduction and height invariant had already been separately audited in repository reviews; I checked their use here, not the original PDFs anew.

The subsequence step has correct quantifiers. For each coordinate prime exponent, an infinite sequence has either a constant infinite subsequence or a subsequence tending to infinity. After three refinements, the minimax exponent tends to infinity by fixed-signature Darmon–Granville finiteness, so at least one coordinate exponent diverges. The bounds `R≤xyz<C^σ` and `h(j)=6log C+O(1)` give the stated slopes because `R` and the odd conductor `N_odd=R/2` tend to infinity by fixed-support S-unit finiteness. In the all-three-diverging profile `σ→0`, the notation `R=C^o(1)` is justified by `0≤log R/log C≤σ→0`.

The signed fixed-support lemma extends the earlier positive-output lemma correctly. For fixed `P,Q` other than `(2,2)`, the affine curve `u^P+εv^Q=D` is smooth and has the displayed genus at least one, including for `ε=-1`. Its rational S-integral points are finite by Siegel. Holding `(u,v,D)` fixed, primitivity at each `q∈S` bounds `v_q(w)` because both input-base valuations would otherwise become positive. This bounds `w` and the distinct powered triples. For an input coordinate with varying exponent, moving terms across the equality yields exactly such a signed equation with the other two exponents fixed.

Two presentation cautions do not change the result. First, the assertion about the largest prime factor of the varying base requires a hypothetical **infinite** family and the convention that the largest prime factor of 1 is undefined; the lemma itself shows unit-base values occur only finitely often in that profile. Second, `R=C^o(1)` and the height slopes are sequence statements and cannot be promoted to universal inequalities for all individual solutions.

The proposed closure still requires a theorem controlling the moving support or an unconditional sufficiently strong height/conductor upper bound. The reviewed inequalities provide neither. The note correctly labels its outcome incomplete and does not call for Lean formalization.
