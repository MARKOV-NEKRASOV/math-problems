# Independent review of Gaussian descent for `(2,4,ℓ)`

**Verdict:** The parity, Gaussian coprimality, unit absorption, polynomial identity, and gcd/square dichotomy in `full-gaussian-descent.md` are correct. The argument is a necessary-condition reduction, not a proof that the family is empty or finite.

1. If `x,y` are odd, `x²+y⁴≡2 (mod 4)`, while an odd-prime power `z^ℓ` has either 2-adic valuation 0 or at least `ℓ≥7`; contradiction. If both are even, primitivity fails. Thus they have opposite parity and `z` is odd.
2. Put `α=x+i y²`. A Gaussian prime dividing both `α` and its conjugate divides `2x` and `2iy²`. Away from the prime `1+i` above 2, this forces its rational prime below to divide both `x` and `y`, impossible. Also `1+i` divides `m+ni` precisely when `m,n` have the same parity; here `x` and `y²` differ in parity. Therefore the factors are coprime. Unique factorization gives `α=ε γ^ℓ`. Because odd `ℓ` is coprime to 4, exponentiation by `ℓ` permutes the unit group, so `ε` can be absorbed into `γ=a+bi`. Taking norms gives `z=a²+b²`. Any common rational prime dividing `a,b` would divide both `x` and `y`, hence `gcd(a,b)=1`; odd `z` forces opposite parity of `a,b`.
3. The displayed binomial formula for `U_ℓ=Im((a+bi)^ℓ)/b` is exact. Modulo `b`, `U_ℓ≡ℓ a^(ℓ−1)`. Since `gcd(a,b)=1`, a common divisor of `b,U_ℓ` can only involve `ℓ`. If `ℓ|b`, then `ℓ∤a`; the `j=1` term has valuation 1, the intermediate odd-`j` terms each have valuation at least 3 (their binomial coefficient is divisible by `ℓ` and `b^(j−1)` contains at least `b²`), and the final `j=ℓ` term has valuation at least `ℓ−1≥6`. Therefore `v_ℓ(U_ℓ)=1` and `gcd(|b|,|U_ℓ|)=gcd(|b|,ℓ)`.
4. Because `y²=bU_ℓ>0`, the two factors have the same sign. If `ℓ∤b`, coprimality makes each absolute factor a square. If `ℓ|b`, the exact valuation 1 in `U_ℓ` makes its absolute value `ℓ` times a square and forces the same square class for `b`, giving the stated `δ` dichotomy. The condition `ℓ∤w` follows from `v_ℓ(U_ℓ)=1`.

The note correctly declines to infer that a primitive divisor of the Lucas quantity has odd valuation. Existence of a prime divisor alone cannot prevent `U_ℓ` from being a square or `ℓ` times one. The free parameters and unbounded prime index remain; moreover, this route does not cover the all-prime signature class. No full conjecture proof follows.
