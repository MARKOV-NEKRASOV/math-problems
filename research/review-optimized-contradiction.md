# Independent review of the infinite-family contradiction attempt

**Verdict:** The diagonal escape, radical calculation and `1/1722` exponent are correct. The argument produces a conditional obstruction, not a proof of Fermat–Catalan.

1. **Simultaneous escape.** For bounded `C`, only finitely many positive powered-value triples `A+B=C` exist. For each finite exponent bound `M`, the canonical exponent set contributes only finitely many signatures, each with finitely many primitive solutions by Darmon–Granville. For each prime bound `M`, `S={primes≤M}` is finite, and the S-unit theorem gives finitely many `A/C+B/C=1` pairs; pairwise coprimality makes the positive triple uniquely recoverable. A finite union of these three exceptional sets can be removed for every integer `i`. Thus one may select distinct solutions with `C_i>i`, `P_i>i` and no canonical representation whose three exponents are all ≤`i`. For `i>25`, at least one exponent in every reduced representation is a prime exceeding `i`. This is not an infinite-union fallacy; it correctly extracts a diagonal sequence under the hypothetical infinite-family assumption.
2. **Radical/abc arithmetic.** Pairwise coprimality gives `R=rad(xyz)`, and `R≤xyz<C^σ≤C^(41/42)`. Hence `C>R^(42/41)`. With `ε=1/42`, `1+ε=43/42`, and `42/41−43/42=(1764−1763)/1722=1/1722`. Thus `C/R^(1+ε)>R^(1/1722)`. Since `P_i|R_i` and `P_i→∞`, the right side diverges. This would violate a single abc inequality with a constant uniform over coprime triples. The unproved abc conjecture is exactly the absent ingredient; the calculation cannot be promoted to an unconditional contradiction.
3. **Scope of the escape property.** The maximum canonical prime exponent grows, not necessarily the minimum exponent or all three exponents. Likewise the largest supporting prime grows, not necessarily the number of supporting primes. The note maintains these distinctions. Fixed-signature and fixed-support finiteness cannot bound the union of varying cases.
