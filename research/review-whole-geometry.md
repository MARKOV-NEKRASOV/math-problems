# Independent review of the geometry/function-field attempt

**Verdict:** The polynomial Mason–Stothers argument is correct under the stated characteristic-zero, nonzero-polynomial, primitive hypotheses. The note correctly stops before claiming an integer proof. I found no invalid transfer or hidden uniformity conclusion.

1. From `x^p+y^q=z^r`, any irreducible factor shared by two bases divides the third, so `gcd(x,y,z)=1` forces pairwise coprimality. This also makes `A=x^p`, `B=y^q`, `C=z^r` pairwise coprime. If any base is nonconstant, `D=max(deg A,deg B,deg C)>0`, as required by the nonconstant case of Mason–Stothers.
2. The polynomial abc bound `D≤deg rad(ABC)−1` holds in characteristic zero. Because powers add no new distinct irreducible factors, `deg rad(ABC)≤deg x+deg y+deg z`. Each degree is at most `D` divided by its exponent, so `D≤Dσ−1<D` for `σ<1`, a contradiction. Constants cause no problem; their degree is zero. The argument actually works when `σ≤1`, but the stated weaker hyperbolic result is correct. Algebraic closure of `k` is stronger than necessary, not an error.
3. The integer abc step uses `rad(ABC)<C^σ≤C^(41/42)` correctly. To obtain `C≤K_ε C^((41/42)(1+ε))` with an exponent strictly below one requires `ε<1/41`; the note has the exact threshold. This step is conditional on the unproved number-field abc conjecture. Mason–Stothers does not supply it for integer constants.
4. The warning about interpolation is valid: infinitely many isolated integer solutions across varying signatures would not produce a nonconstant polynomial solution. Fixed-signature finiteness, even with a uniform per-curve point count, cannot bound the union across infinitely many curves. The sample curve `v²=u^(2g+1)+1` is squarefree in characteristic zero, has genus `g`, and has point `(0,1)` for every `g≥1`, so it correctly refutes a genus-only vanishing shortcut.

**Minor precision:** The displayed `Dσ−1<D` uses `D>0` and `σ<1`, both already stated. The theorem's `k` algebraically closed assumption is unnecessary but makes the setting unambiguous. The missing number-field height or exponent-uniformity theorem is genuinely the unresolved step; no full Fermat–Catalan proof follows.
