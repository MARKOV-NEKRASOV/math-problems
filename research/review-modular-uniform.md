# Independent review: varying (2,3,p) modular route

**Verdict:** The deductions in `research/attempt-modular-uniform.md` are sound as a partial route. They do not prove Fermat–Catalan. I checked the claimed paper results against Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll, arXiv:1703.05058v3, and Freitas–Mocanu–Sánchez-Rodríguez, arXiv:2512.10781v1.

## Checks against the sources

1. For `E: v²=u³+3bu−2a`, direct calculation gives `Δ=−2⁶3³(a²+b³)=−2⁶3³c^p`, `c₄=−2⁴3²b`, and `j=1728 b³/c^p`. This matches [Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll, (2.2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05058). For positive `a,b,c`, `0<b³<c^p`, so `0<j<1728`. The seven fixed curves and twist set `{±1,±2,±3,±6}` are exactly Lemma 2.3, conditional there on irreducible mod-`p` Galois representation and `c≠0`.
2. Proposition 2.4 proves irreducibility for **nontrivial primitive integer solutions** when prime `p≥11`; the following remark attributes `p=7` to Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll. The note's sentence about irreducibility is correct in its positive-solution setting, but should include “nontrivial primitive” and identify the `p=7` statement as a cited result.
3. [Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.05058) has four to ten surviving signed twists depending on `p mod 24`; **the table explicitly applies only for `p≥11`**. It lists twists that cannot *yet* be ruled out as giving a nontrivial primitive solution. It does not assert any of them has a rational point, or that every rational point reconstructs to an integer solution. Add this qualification to the note.
4. The signed solution `(a,b,c)=(±3,−2,1)` satisfies the equation for every prime `p`; the paper identifies its Frey curve with a twist of `864b1` after Lemma 2.3. It is outside the positive-base conjecture. Its existence demonstrates that residual-representation elimination alone cannot discard every candidate class; one must use positivity or reconstruction data. The real interval on `j` is necessary for positive solutions, but a torsion isomorphism to a fixed curve says nothing about equality of their `j` values.
5. [Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05058) classifies nontrivial primitive integer solutions for `p=11` **assuming GRH**. The paper's introduction says the GRH dependency enters its class-group computation in degree-36 fields. Its partial `p=13` work and the `p=7` classification do not establish a uniform result.
6. [Freitas–Mocanu–Sánchez-Rodríguez, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10781) excludes **antisymplectic** twists for its listed `(E,p)` pairs, all with `E` among `864a1`, `864b1`, `864c1`. Theorem 3.1 concerns only Table 1 pairs: if `X_E^−(p)(Q_q)` is nonempty at a prime `q>3`, `q≠p`, it contains a point arising from a `q`-adic primitive solution. This is a local statement and does not give a global signed or positive solution. The note characterizes this correctly.

## Concrete next obligation

Target the `27a1` branch of Table 4 for primes `p≥17` with `p≡2 (mod 3)`. Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll Lemma 6.1 eliminates its rational points arising from nontrivial primitive solutions when `p≡1 (mod 3)`, but explicitly says an analogous non-split-Cartan result is unavailable for the remaining case. Prove that no rational point on the surviving `X_{27a1}^±(p)` twists can reconstruct to **positive** primitive `a²+b³=c^p`, perhaps using the necessary `0<j<1728` plus integrality and local conditions. This would remove one infinite branch; it would remain a partial result, since the other six curve branches and other exponent signatures persist.
