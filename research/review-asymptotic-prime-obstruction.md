# Independent review of the asymptotic all-prime obstruction

**Verdict:** The deduction in `asymptotic-prime-obstruction.md` is sound as a necessary consequence of a hypothetical infinite Fermat–Catalan family. It does not give an unconditional contradiction.

1. **Representation quantifier.** After discarding the finite composite-containing part, each powered-value triple has at least one eligible all-prime representation. The minimum `M(T)` of its largest exponent exists even if representations are nonunique. For any fixed `H`, only finitely many such triples have `M(T)≤H`: finitely many ordered prime signatures lie in `[2,H]^3`, and Darmon–Granville gives finitely many primitive solutions per fixed hyperbolic signature. Thus `M(T)→∞` along any sequence of distinct remaining triples. A unit base can create arbitrarily many exponent representations, but Mihăilescu leaves only finitely many distinct unit-base powered triples, so it cannot support the infinite family.
2. **Radical exponent.** In the representation attaining `M(T)`, the other two exponents cannot both be 2 because the signature would have reciprocal sum at least 1. For primes, their maximum reciprocal sum is `1/2+1/3=5/6`. Hence `σ_T≤5/6+1/M(T)`, and `R=rad(ABC)≤xyz<C^σ_T`. The `o(1)` is sequence-specific; no pointwise exponent `5/6` is claimed.
3. **Conductor and height.** The exact invariant calculation `h(j)=6log C+O(1)` is uniform. Odd bad primes of the Frey curve are exactly those of `ABC`, each with conductor exponent 1. The 2-adic exponent has an absolute bound, so `N≤K₂R`. If `N` were bounded along infinitely many triples, prime support would lie in a fixed finite set, contradicting fixed-`S` S-unit finiteness. Therefore `N→∞`; combining `log N≤(5/6+o(1))log C+O(1)` with `h(j)=6log C+O(1)` yields `h(j)≥(36/5−o(1))log N`. Using the odd conductor `N_odd=R/2` avoids reliance on a sharp 2-adic numerical constant and gives the same slope.
4. **Conditional abc threshold.** For each fixed `ε<1/5`, choose `δ>0` with `(5/6+δ)(1+ε)<1`. Eventually `R<C^(5/6+δ)`, so `C/R^(1+ε)>C^[1−(5/6+δ)(1+ε)]→∞` as `C→∞`. This would violate a uniform abc bound at that `ε`, but no such bound is established. At `ε=1/5` the displayed exponent no longer stays positive. The strict threshold is correct.

No full proof follows: an upper height–conductor inequality with slope below `36/5` or an abc-type upper radical inequality with `ε<1/5` is exactly the missing uniform input.
