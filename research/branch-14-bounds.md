# B14-W: a necessary Wieferich congruence at a residual input prime

**30 October 2026; PROVED elementary necessary condition; no F2 closure.** Let `r>=3` be fixed, `p>r` an odd prime, and let `a,b,c` be positive integers with `gcd(a,b,c)=1` and `a²+b^r=c^p`. If `p|b`, then `a^(p-1)≡1 (mod p²)`. If `p|a`, then `b^(p-1)≡1 (mod p²)`.

**Proof.** Primitivity makes the three bases pairwise coprime, so `p|b` implies `p∤ac`; and `p|a` implies `p∤bc`. In the first case, `b^r≡0 (mod p²)`, hence `a²≡c^p (mod p²)`. Raise to `p−1`. By Euler's theorem `c^{p(p−1)}≡1 (mod p²)`, whence `a^{2(p−1)}≡1 (mod p²)`. Fermat's theorem writes `a^{p−1}=1+kp (mod p²)`; its square is `1+2kp`, and oddness of p forces `k≡0 (mod p)`. In the second case, `a²≡0 (mod p²)` and `b^r≡c^p (mod p²)`. Raising to `p−1` gives `b^{r(p−1)}≡1`. Write `b^{p−1}=1+kp`; then `rk≡0 (mod p)`. Since `p>r`, `k≡0 (mod p)`. □

**Sharpness and example.** For each p, `t^{p−1}≡1 (mod p²)` has exactly `p−1` unit residue classes modulo `p²`, one lift of each nonzero residue modulo p by Hensel's lemma. Thus the condition is restrictive but compatible with arbitrary p; e.g. `t≡1 (mod p²)` meets it. The known primitive positive solution `2213459²+1414³=65⁷` has `p=7|1414`, and direct computation gives `2213459^6≡1 (mod 49)`. This verifies that `p|b` occurs in the actual hyperbolic problem and cannot be excluded wholesale. Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll, *Twists of X(7)*, arXiv:math/0508174v1, Theorem 1.1 (as source-checked in `research/review-congruence.md`) lists this solution.

**Prior bound → new restriction → gap.** Previously the `p|ab` class lacked even this explicit local condition. It now lies on the moving Fermat-quotient-zero residue classes of the other input base. This imposes no upper bound on p, c, or the number/size of prime factors of c; it does not prove F2 `(2,r,p)` finite or transfer to F1/F0. A hypothetical upper `log C≤κ log rad(abc)+o(p)`, `κ<2r/(r+2)`, is still absent. The condition depends on p modulo p², while the field `Q(ζ_r)^+` used in the modular method has degree `(r−1)/2` and its constants may grow when r varies.
