# Bound dashboard and targeted proof obligations for all-prime Fermat–Catalan

**Scope.** Let `A=x^p`, `B=y^q`, `C=z^r=A+B` be distinct primitive positive powered-value triples with prime exponents `p,q,r≥2`, `σ=1/p+1/q+1/r<1`, and nonunit bases. Put `R=rad(ABC)`, `M=max(p,q,r)`. This dashboard records **proved necessary conditions**, their directions, and the additional statements that would actually eliminate an infinite family. None of the additional statements is asserted as proved.

## Which next lemma would materially raise an exclusion threshold?

1. **A global branch-closing lemma for positive `(2,3,p)`.** Prove that for one specified surviving FNS curve, no positive primitive Frey solution realizes its residual representation for `p>P`. This would eliminate an infinite exponent family for that curve, unlike adding a single forbidden small prime. The `27a1` non-split branch with `p≡2 (mod 3)` is a precise target. The finite-trace and output-roughness conditions are starting invariants; converting them into isogeny without the global equation is invalid, as the project's explicit Frey-shaped counterexample shows.
2. **An independently validated simultaneous-factor theorem for `(p,p,t)`.** A corrected and verified nonunit cofactor exclusion for `p≥5`, or a descent that uses both `U` and `V`, would remove infinitely many repeated-prime signatures. The p=3 branch and distinct-prime branches would remain. For the positive clean branch, any proposed upper bound on `u` must beat the proven lower rate `t^(1/(p−1))` to produce a contradiction.
3. **A moving-parameter strengthening of signed fixed-gap finiteness.** The present theorem proves finiteness for each fixed `P,Q,D`; it does not control the union as `D` varies. An explicit bound on `|D|` for all primitive solutions with fixed `P,Q` would finish that one-diverging-exponent profile. This is a concrete arithmetic-geometric target, though no such bound follows from Siegel or Schinzel–Tijdeman as currently used.

Graph-theoretic packaging of the modular selectors is useful as a bookkeeping/sieve device only if a mathematical lemma forces every solution into an incompatible pair of selector sets. The mere existence of a finite graph of small-prime conditions is not an exclusion theorem. Likewise, stronger lower bounds on prime size or base size do not contradict the conjectural family's possibility unless paired with a proved upper bound.

No complete Fermat–Catalan proof is claimed; Lean formalization remains deferred.
