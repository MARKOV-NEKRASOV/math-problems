# Independent review of fixed-input-exponent, fixed-output-support finiteness

**Verdict:** I find the lemma in `fixed-output-support.md` correct for fixed integers `P,Q≥2` other than `(2,2)`, fixed finite prime set `S`, positive primitive solutions, and variable integer output exponent `r≥2`. It is restricted finiteness, not a full Fermat–Catalan proof.

1. **Normalization.** If `supp(c)⊆S`, then `C=c^r` is supported on `S`. Division of each `q`-adic exponent of `C` by `L=lcm(P,Q)` uniquely yields `C=Dw^L` with `D` from at most `L^{|S|}` choices and `w` a positive `S`-unit integer. Because `L/P,L/Q` are integers, `u=a/w^{L/P}` and `v=b/w^{L/Q}` are rational `S`-integers and satisfy `u^P+v^Q=D` exactly.
2. **Curve and genus.** For `D≠0`, the affine curve is smooth since both partial derivatives vanish only at `u=v=0`, which is not a point. To check the projective genus, view `u^P=D-v^Q` as a degree-`P` cover of the `v`-line. The `Q` finite zeros of `D-v^Q` are simple and totally ramified. Over infinity there are `d=gcd(P,Q)` points, each with ramification index `P/d`. Riemann–Hurwitz gives `2g−2=−2P+Q(P−1)+(P−d)`, or `g=((P−1)(Q−1)−(d−1))/2`. This is at least 1 for all `P,Q≥2` except `(2,2)`. The curve is geometrically integral because `D-v^Q` has simple zeros and cannot be a nontrivial perfect power, so the cover/genus calculation is legitimate. Siegel's theorem then gives finitely many `S`-integral points for each of the finitely many `D`.
3. **Bounding the scaling factor.** For a fixed `(u,v,D)` and `q∈S`, put `t=v_q(w)`. When `t>0`, `q|c` because `q|C=c^r`. The valuations of `a,b` are `v_q(u)+(L/P)t` and `v_q(v)+(L/Q)t`, both nonnegative integers. Primitivity prevents both being positive, so at least one equals zero. That bounds `t` in terms of the fixed valuations of `u,v`. Each prime in finite `S` has only finitely many possible `t`; hence only finitely many `w`, integers `a,b`, and powered triples. Unit bases cause no exception, as the same valuations and positivity apply.

**Scope correction to avoid:** Bounded output support is stronger than bounded number of output prime factors; the latter lets the primes move. Likewise the all-prime signature reduction does not fix `P,Q`. The note keeps these limitations explicit. The generalized lemma supplies no unconditional fixed support or fixed input pair for a hypothetical infinite family, so it cannot close the conjecture.
