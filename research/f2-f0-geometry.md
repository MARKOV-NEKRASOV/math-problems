## A12-G: extension and audit of the three-point barrier

**State: PROVED elementary implication; the perfectness input is already recorded in triangle-group literature.** Let `X→P¹_C` be any connected degree-`n` cover branched at three points, and suppose its three local monodromy permutations have exact orders `e1,e2,e3>1` that are pairwise coprime. The original cover need **not** be Galois. Then its monodromy group `G≤S_n` is perfect and

`e1 e2 e3 | |G| ≤ n!`.                                           (G′)

**Proof.** The connected covering's monodromy is transitive and generated by permutations `g1,g2,g3` of exact orders `e1,e2,e3`, with `g1g2g3=1`. In `G_ab`, the class of `g1` equals the inverse product of the classes of `g2,g3`; its order divides both `e1` and `lcm(e2,e3)`, hence is one. The same holds cyclically, so `G_ab=1`. By Lagrange each `ei` divides `|G|`, and pairwise coprimality gives their product divides `|G|`. The monodromy action is faithful by definition, so `|G|≤n!`. **Only the perfectness conclusion needs** the exactly-three-point product relation and generation. The factorial degree bound itself still holds for any faithful degree-`n` permutation group containing three elements of exact pairwise-coprime orders, even if a cover has additional branch points. The Galois closure of the original three-point cover has the same three local monodromy orders and obeys the original [Theorem G](F2_TO_F0_GEOMETRIC_BARRIER.md) genus formula. That genus conclusion does not automatically apply to the non-Galois quotient `X` itself. (G′) supplies a direct degree obstruction for `X`.

**Old bound → new bound → threshold.** The previous note controlled genus only for Galois covers encoding exact coprime indices. (G′) adds `n!≥e1e2e3` for a non-Galois cover with the same exact monodromy orders. For distinct prime indices with minimum `m→∞`, `n!≥m³`, so bounded cover degree is impossible. However, each permutation's order already divides `n!`, and for a prime local order `ei` its action requires `n≥ei`; thus `n≥max(ei)` is a stronger, immediate degree lower bound in the prime-index application. The genuinely additional conclusion is perfect monodromy. All `ei` here mean the exact orders of the *whole local permutations*, not ramification orders at selected points. This is a design filter, **not** an upper bound on `C=z^r` or an exclusion of Fermat–Catalan solutions. It does not touch motives of fixed rank whose local monodromy has an infinite-order component or whose indices do not equal all three exponents; in the cited rank-two HGM design the three local monodromy types are `{q,∞,r}`, so the finite-three-index hypothesis fails. Conder–Young, arXiv:2410.06571v1, introduction p.2 and §3, already states/performs the pairwise-coprime triangle-group perfectness calculation; (G′) is an immediate faithful-permutation corollary, not claimed original.

## A12-F: fixed rank does not fix the field in the cited HGM construction

**State: PROVED field-dependence lemma for the specified construction.** Let `q,r` be distinct odd primes and let `K_s=Q(ζ_s+ζ_s^{-1})`. The rank-two hypergeometric motives used for signature `(q,r,p)` in Cazorla García–Villagra Torcomian, arXiv:2503.21568v2, Section 6.4, and Golfieri Madriaga–Pacetti, arXiv:2412.08804v2, are presented over `F=K_q K_r`. Then

`[F:Q]=(q−1)(r−1)/4`,

`|disc(F)|=q^((q−3)(r−1)/4) r^((r−3)(q−1)/4)`.           (F)

**Proof.** `K_s` is the maximal real subfield of the prime cyclotomic field, so `[K_s:Q]=(s−1)/2`. The conductor–discriminant formula gives `|disc(K_s)|=s^((s−3)/2)`: the `(s−3)/2` nontrivial even characters modulo `s` each have conductor `s`. The fields `K_q` and `K_r` have disjoint finite ramification and hence intersection `Q` (a nontrivial common field would be unramified at every finite prime, impossible over `Q`). Their degrees multiply. Their discriminants are coprime, so the discriminant of the compositum is `disc(K_q)^[K_r:Q] disc(K_r)^[K_q:Q]`, yielding (F). Equivalently, take the product of the even-character groups and apply conductor–discriminant directly. This checks q=3 as the trivial `K_3=Q` edge.

**Old bound → new bound → threshold.** The cited HGM has rank two, but its chosen totally real base field has degree growing like `qr/4` and log-discriminant

`((q−3)(r−1)/4) log q + ((r−3)(q−1)/4) log r`.

If `m=min(p,q,r)→∞` in F0, then `q,r≥m`, so in particular

`log|disc(F)| ≥ ((m−3)(m−1)/2) log m`.

This is larger than the protocol's allowable additive error `E(m)=o(m log m)` by a factor growing at least linearly in `m`. Hence an attempted F0 upper bound whose uncancelled additive term is a fixed positive multiple of `log|disc(F)|` cannot meet Objective U. This is a **test of an estimate's dependence**, not a claim that every possible estimate must pay such a term.

There is also a simple descent diagnostic. For a two-dimensional representation `ρ` of `G_F`, the naively induced representation `Ind_(G_F)^(G_Q) ρ` has dimension `2[F:Q]=(q−1)(r−1)/2` over its coefficient field. If an abelian variety `A/F` underlies the construction, `Res_(F/Q) A` has dimension `[F:Q] dim A`. Thus this standard restriction-of-scalars operation trades the growing base field for growing rank/dimension; it cannot be described as simultaneously fixed rank and fixed base field. This does **not** rule out a different, more subtle descent or an unrelated fixed-rank representation over `Q`.

For **F2** with `q,r` fixed and residual `p` varying, these field parameters are fixed. For **F1** with one of `q,r` diverging, at least one degree/discriminant parameter grows. For **F0** with both diverging, both grow. Thus a constant from Hilbert modularity, Chebotarev, an isogeny estimate, or level lowering over `F` cannot be treated as uniform in F1/F0 just because the representation has rank two. (F) is a property of the *field used by these papers*, not a proof that every possible representation must use a field of this degree or that this field is minimal. This route has not produced the protocol's needed upper bound `log C≤((1−δ)m/3)log R+o(m log m)` for F0, or its F2/F1 radical thresholds.
