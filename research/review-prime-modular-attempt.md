# Independent review of fixed-curve modular uniformity attempt

**Verdict:** The conditional deduction for `(2,3,ℓ)` is mathematically sound under its explicitly hypothetical fixed-curve Frey–Mazur premise. That premise is not established, and the route does not cover all-prime signatures in general. No full Fermat–Catalan proof follows.

I checked Freitas–Naskręcki–Stoll, arXiv:1703.05058v3, Lemma 2.3 and Proposition 2.4 against the available primary PDF text. For primitive nontrivial integer `a²+b³=c^p`, Lemma 2.3 applies for prime `p≥7` **provided** the Frey mod-`p` representation is irreducible; it produces a twist by `d∈{±1,±2,±3,±6}` whose `p`-torsion matches one of exactly seven listed rational elliptic curves. Proposition 2.4 supplies irreducibility for prime `p≥11`. The note respects these hypotheses. The fixed prime `p=7` is separately known from Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll and is irrelevant to the proposed uniform bound.

For `E:y²=x³+3bx−2a`, direct Weierstrass formulas give `Δ=−2⁶3³(a²+b³)=−2⁶3³c^p`, `c₄=−2⁴3²b`, and `j=1728b³/c^p`. Primitivity plus the equation gives `gcd(b,c)=1`. Thus the denominator of reduced `j` is exactly `c^p/gcd(c^p,1728)`, and for positive nonunit `c≥2` it is at least `2^p/1728`. Quadratic twisting preserves `j`.

Assume, *hypothetically*, a single constant `L` such that for `p>L`, any rational elliptic curve with mod-`p` representation isomorphic to one of the seven fixed curves is rationally isogenous to that curve. Applying it to the finite twists from Lemma 2.3 places their `j`-invariants in finitely many values: a fixed rational isogeny class has finitely many `Q`-isomorphism classes. The denominator lower bound would then bound `p`, and fixed-signature Darmon–Granville finiteness would finish `(2,3,p)`. The implication is valid; the fixed-curve Frey–Mazur assertion is **unproved** here. It must not be reported as a theorem. It also addresses only one infinite subfamily, while the all-prime reduction retains other moving-prime signatures.

The warning about the universal Frey curve is appropriately narrow. At an odd prime `t|ABC`, `c₄` is a unit and `v_t(Δ_min)=2v_t(ABC)`, so a selected residual prime removes that base prime from the level by exponent divisibility only for coordinates whose exponent is a multiple of the selected modulus (apart from accidental base valuations). This explains why the single-modulus route does not automatically lower all three moving supports in pairwise-distinct-prime cases; it does not rule out other modular strategies.
